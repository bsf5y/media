## You're already a Senior/Principle/Lead developer

Sure, you remember grafting late at night over Uncle Bob books and hacking away at experimental
code.  Been there done that, and have the badges to prove it.  But now you're at the top of your
game, so that stuff is well and truely behind you.  Why should you bother learning a whole new
paradigm now?  You're already set, and it's almost 4:30.

## Processes and toolchains just get in the way

You know, you've heard it all before.  The wacky CI/CD fever, linting facists,
and test-all-the-things lunatics. Non of that nonsense was necessary then and
it certainly isn't necessary now.  You've been emailing firmware updates to the
support team for ages and it's never been a problem.  The AI fan-boys can just
go spin.

## Your floppy disks have a larger context window

Let's face it.  So-called 'frontier' AI models are peaking out at 1M context
windows.  That's pathetic.  You have a drawer full of 1.44M floppy disks prove
it.  Besides, the biggest source file in your codebase is only 800k so what
does it matter.

## Failure is where all the fun is

"A man works hard for his filth just to have vagrants come and steal it." - *George Liquor, 1991*  I mean really.  It's
taken you decades to figure out how to debug this stuff.  That's how you add value.  There's no AI
built that can understand the kind of breakage you have to tolerate in your codebase:

  [EventHorizon]) -> Result<FluxField, EntropyViolation>>, but received an unholy invocation of
  std::mem::transmute::<&'a str, &'b mut CosmicRadiation>. 

## Product managers and sales just can't handle the truth

Have you met those folks.  They can barely convince people to buy our products
as it is.  We can only imagine what circle of hell would open if features and
functionalty started flowing like water.  Besides that, we all know that
customers are best kept wishing for more.
